HAPTICS: FOUNDATIONS, INTERFACES, AND PERCEPTION
PRIVATE COURSE SPECIFICATION

PURPOSE
Create a graduate-level open course that explains haptics from first principles through advanced research. The course connects human touch perception, hardware and actuation, rendering and control, psychophysics, computational modelling, experiment design, system evaluation, and emerging research frontiers.

STANDARD CHAPTER FORMAT
Every chapter should contain:
- chapter title and subtitle;
- course part, difficulty, reading time, prerequisites, status, and version;
- learning objectives;
- intuitive explanation before formalism;
- formal definitions and physical or mathematical model;
- design implications;
- worked example;
- common misconceptions;
- limitations and open questions;
- key takeaways;
- five-question self-test;
- practical or research-design exercise;
- recommended reading;
- citation and revision information.

CONTENT MODEL
- Long-form chapters are Markdown.
- Chapter, reference, glossary, pathway, and exercise metadata are CSV.
- Each chapter is located by a marker such as <!-- CHAPTER:01 -->.
- Chapter metadata specifies the associated Markdown content file.
- Pipe characters separate multi-value CSV fields.

CONTENT QUALITY REQUIREMENTS
- Define terminology consistently.
- Separate actuator command, physical output, and perceptual response.
- State assumptions, units, body site, mounting, and operating conditions.
- Use equations only when they clarify the concept.
- Include calibrated physical measurement and perceptual validation where relevant.
- Distinguish established findings from hypotheses and open research questions.
- Use primary sources and standard textbooks.
- Avoid unsupported universal claims from one device, body site, task, or population.
- Include safety notes for mechanical, acoustic, electrical, thermal, and clinical systems.
- Include accessible figure descriptions and licensing information.
